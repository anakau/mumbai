RiskSense
On 28 March 2026, we ran the first Aamchi Mumbai Citizen Risk Clinic, a 2.5-hour participatory workshop to map how Mumbaikars respond to systemic climate risks in their city, convened as part of Mumbai Climate Week 2026's Spoke Events. The room brought together organisations working with residents, gig workers, students, teachers, domestic workers, and practitioners working across climate, governance, social protection, and community organising.

The clinic was held in the aftermath of an extraordinary 2025–26 climate year for Mumbai — a March heat wave that pushed felt temperatures past 47°C, followed by the earliest monsoon in 75 years and 500mm of rainfall in four days in August. We chose this moment deliberately: the city's lived memory of cascading climate risk was active, recent, and unsettled.
To test whether citizen risk intelligence can be surfaced, organised, and made legible as a civic asset. Rather than treating citizen input as testimony, the clinic treated citizen knowledge as the continuous capacity of populations to detect weak signals, adapt behaviour under stress, transmit survival knowledge socially, and update their understanding of how systems fail in real time.

We posit that citizens are already functioning as a distributed risk sensing infrastructure for the city of Mumbai.
We chose cyclical flooding and heatwaves as the macro frame of Mumbai's systemic risk field, on the basis that these are the conditions under which the city's systemic failures become most legible to all its local residents across class, caste, gender, infrastructure, and labour.

Field Hunches
List
  • Climate risk in Mumbai is chronic, not episodic. It is embodied, continuous, longitudinal, and carried in lived memory.
  • The most exposed citizens are not passive, but are forced to adapt at any cost because of class, caste, location, invisibility in the system, and Mumbai's urban identity as an expensive city of dreams that people come to at any cost to succeed.
  • Vulnerability is not a characteristic of people. It is produced by structural choices (such as loan structures, rental enforcement, political power differentials, the language of governance, etc.) and deepens cyclically.
  • Citizens already hold deep insight into the risks they live with, held in their minds, bodies, and social networks. What is missing is the institutional flow back in the form of formal information, subsidies, insurance, anticipatory support articulated in multiple languages, designed for their needs, and accessible before crisis hits.
  • Risks are compounded by their interaction with each other. Single-issue risk tools actively misrepresent that compounding by making each risk appear manageable in isolation.
  • Long social cascades (such as identity erosion, intergenerational trauma, slow breakdown of dignity and routine) accumulate silently.
  • The city may appear functional because vulnerable populations continuously absorb instability through individual coping mechanisms, and create the illusion of resilience.

The experience of managing health, housing, and academic continuity under climate stress produces intelligence that formal systems cannot see.

Mould mapping Which buildings and flats develop mould during monsoon and how quickly damp enters after flooding
Signal
Street-level respiratory health data that satellite imagery cannot see. Can inform ward-level housing inspection schedules and preventive maintenance mandates before mould becomes a health crisis.
Landlord accountability gaps How informal rental arrangements and absent tenancy law leave residents unable to demand climate-related repairs
Signal
Evidence base for climate-proofing rental housing legislation. A case for habitation standards under monsoon and flood conditions — legally enforceable regardless of lease type.
Health-hiding behaviour When and why residents conceal illness to protect housing stability, family relationships, and the right to remain in the city
Signal
Where formal support systems are invisible or inaccessible to migrants — the design gap in city health navigation for non-domiciled residents. Points to the need for confidential, migrant-accessible health navigation that does not trigger family or housing consequences.
Cost of staying well The out-of-pocket spending required to maintain basic health in an exposed rental: masks, dehumidifiers, medicines, visits
Signal
A household-level climate burden invisible to macro datasets. Evidence for scholarship extensions, rental subsidies, and climate adaptation stipends for students in informal housing.
Power-cut patterns When outages occur during climate events, for how long, and how they compound health and academic continuity simultaneously
Signal
Temporal data on infrastructure failure that disrupts both cooling and digital access at once. Inputs for ward-level grid resilience planning and academic continuity policy that treats power access as a climate equity issue.
Informal peer care The mutual aid system that moves health information, medicines, and support among students and migrants without institutional backing
Signal
Trust-based distributed care already operating under stress. Investing in peer health navigators can activate existing infrastructure rather than building new institutions from scratch.
Body as record The cumulative physical record of climate exposure held in the body: lungs, asthma history, seasonal patterns, accumulated stress
Signal
Longitudinal health data that formal medical records do not capture. Basis for climate health entitlements for students and informal urban migrants — occupational-adjacent rights grounded in embodied evidence.
Reverse migration threshold The tipping point at which illness forces return to origin city, losing urban investment, networks, and two years of life built here
Signal
A quantifiable climate risk with a measurable intervention window. Targeted health support, academic accommodation, and housing protections deployed before the threshold is crossed can prevent irreversible migration loss.
Academic disruption mapping How floods, heat, and power cuts interrupt study continuity and accumulate into grade risk and dropout pressure over a semester
Signal
The downstream education cost of climate exposure, term by term. Evidence for climate-resilient academic policy: automatic extensions, health-related deferrals, and inclusive attendance rules triggered by climate events.
