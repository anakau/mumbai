On 28 March 2026, we ran the first Aamchi Mumbai Citizen Risk Clinic, a 2.5-hour participatory workshop to map how Mumbaikars respond to systemic climate risks in their city, convened as part of Mumbai Climate Week 2026's Spoke Events. The room brought together organisations working with residents, gig workers, students, teachers, domestic workers, and practitioners working across climate, governance, social protection, and community organising.

The clinic was held in the aftermath of an extraordinary 2025–26 climate year for Mumbai — a March heat wave that pushed felt temperatures past 47°C, followed by the earliest monsoon in 75 years and 500mm of rainfall in four days in August. We chose this moment deliberately: the city's lived memory of cascading climate risk was active, recent, and unsettled.

To test whether citizen risk intelligence can be surfaced, organised, and made legible as a civic asset. Rather than treating citizen input as testimony, the clinic treated citizen knowledge as the continuous capacity of populations to detect weak signals, adapt behaviour under stress, transmit survival knowledge socially, and update their understanding of how systems fail in real time.

We posit that citizens are already functioning as a distributed risk sensing infrastructure for the city of Mumbai.
We chose cyclical flooding and heatwaves as the macro frame of Mumbai's systemic risk field, on the basis that these are the conditions under which the city's systemic failures become most legible to all its local residents across class, caste, gender, infrastructure, and labour.
Field Hunches
  • Climate risk in Mumbai is chronic, not episodic. It is embodied, continuous, longitudinal, and carried in lived memory.
  • The most exposed citizens are not passive, but are forced to adapt at any cost because of class, caste, location, invisibility in the system, and Mumbai's urban identity as an expensive city of dreams that people come to at any cost to succeed.
  • Vulnerability is not a characteristic of people. It is produced by structural choices (such as loan structures, rental enforcement, political power differentials, the language of governance, etc.) and deepens cyclically.
  • Citizens already hold deep insight into the risks they live with, held in their minds, bodies, and social networks. What is missing is the institutional flow back in the form of formal information, subsidies, insurance, anticipatory support articulated in multiple languages, designed for their needs, and accessible before crisis hits.
  • Risks are compounded by their interaction with each other. Single-issue risk tools actively misrepresent that compounding by making each risk appear manageable in isolation.
  • Long social cascades (such as identity erosion, intergenerational trauma, slow breakdown of dignity and routine) accumulate silently.
  • The city may appear functional because vulnerable populations continuously absorb instability through individual coping mechanisms, and create the illusion of resilience.
Riding 14 hours a day across Mumbai in extreme heat, Nasim accumulates knowledge no satellite or platform algorithm captures. He knows which neighbourhoods run hotter, which streets flood first, which restaurants will let a rider rest. He has mapped, through his body, the city's climate risk in real time.
This is not incidental. It is intelligence — produced daily, at personal cost, and absorbed silently into the platform's operating efficiency. His route adaptations reduce delivery failures. His informal cooling network functions as distributed preventive healthcare. His heat awareness is a live urban sensor the city does not have.

But none of it is recognised, named, or compensated. Nasim is not classified as a knowledge worker. His expertise has no home. The risk he absorbs is the unpriced subsidy of a revenue model calibrated to demand spikes in both heat and flood seasons.

She knows which commute routes flood first and in which sequence. She knows how to keep her nebuliser charged through power cuts. She knows which neighbours share solutions for damp walls and which landlords in her building have acted. She knows how to shift online when the college does not yet know it needs to.

None of this knowledge is recorded. None of it is compensated. It is the daily intelligence of someone who cannot afford to be caught off guard — produced at the cost of her health, her savings, and her silence.
